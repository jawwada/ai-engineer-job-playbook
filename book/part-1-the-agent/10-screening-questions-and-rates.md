# 10. Screening questions, US work authorization and rates

Most rejections in the US market happen before a human reads the resume, on the screening questions. The rule for the agent is simple and non-negotiable: every answer comes from the answers bank or the truth file, and a question it cannot map is handed to you. The rule for you is to know the vocabulary well enough to fill the bank correctly.

**Work authorization, in the words the forms use.** *"Are you legally authorized to work in the United States?"* is Yes for citizens, green-card holders, and anyone holding a valid work visa or EAD (H-1B, L-1, O-1, TN, OPT/STEM OPT, H-4 EAD). *"Will you now or in the future require sponsorship?"* is No only for citizens and permanent residents; for everyone else the honest answer is Yes, and for H-1B holders the useful phrasing is *"Yes — H-1B transfer"* because a transfer is cheap and fast for the employer compared with a new petition. *"Are you a US citizen or green-card holder?"* is a hard filter on some postings (government, defense, some banks); answer truthfully and let the screener skip those postings in advance. *Security clearance* questions are Yes only if you hold an active clearance. Do not let the agent, or yourself, soften any of these: a wrong answer is discovered at the I-9 stage and ends the offer.

**Employment models and what they mean for you.** *W2* means you are the employer's (or the staffing agency's) employee: taxes withheld, benefits possible, sponsorship possible. *C2C / corp-to-corp* means your own company, or the consulting firm that holds your visa, invoices the client; this is how most H-1B contractors work in the US, and it requires the vendor to be named on the form. *1099* is independent contracting without a corporate layer — not available to H-1B holders. Postings that say *W2 only, no C2C* are a real constraint for visa holders on C2C; the screener flags them so you do not burn a tailored resume. Full-time (FTE) postings for visa holders mean an H-1B transfer at hire; say so in one line in the cover note.

**Rates and salaries.** Forms often force a number. Keep three numbers in the truth file and never let the agent improvise: the hourly C2C rate (which includes the vendor's margin and no benefits — for senior AI engineering in 2026 the contract market sits roughly in the $90–$140/h band for remote C2C), the equivalent W2 hourly (10–20% lower, since the employer pays payroll taxes and benefits), and the full-time base you would accept (senior/staff AI engineer and FDE roles at US product companies generally post $180k–$260k base plus equity; staffing-agency contract roles pay less and often cap the hourly). When a posting states a rate far below your floor the screener skips it; when the form asks for *desired salary* and the range is in the posting, the bank answers at the upper third of the posted range. Write *negotiable* only where there is a free-text field — a numeric field with "0" is read as a mistake.

**Questions the bank should contain** (with your own true answers): authorized to work; sponsorship now or future; citizenship or permanent residency; clearance; W2 or C2C and the vendor's name; current location and willingness to relocate (remote-only candidates answer "No, remote"); years of experience with each named technology (from the skill-years table, never rounded up); highest degree; start date and notice period; desired salary or rate; willingness to travel (give a percentage); whether you have applied to or worked for the company before; how you heard about the role; and the EEO block — gender, race, veteran status, disability — which the bank answers *Decline to self-identify* (it is lawful and has no effect on the application). Consent questions for SMS and marketing are answered No; privacy-policy acknowledgements required to submit are answered Yes only after you approved that company once.

**Red flags the screener uses to skip a posting before you spend time on it**: *US citizens only / USC-GC only*, *W2 only* (for C2C candidates), *no sponsorship* on a full-time role (for visa holders), *onsite* or a specific city with no remote option, *active clearance*, a stated rate under your floor, a posting older than 48 hours, and duplicate postings of the same client role by several agencies. These are logged as `skipped` with the reason, so you can override any of them by hand.
