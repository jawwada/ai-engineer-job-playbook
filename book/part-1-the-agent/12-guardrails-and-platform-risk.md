# 12. Guardrails and platform risk

The agent acts under your name. These rules are written into the kit's `CLAUDE.md` so every session starts with them; read them once so you know what it will refuse to do and why.

**The never list.** The agent never creates accounts, never types a password, never solves or bypasses a CAPTCHA, never edits your LinkedIn profile, never sends an email (drafts only), never ticks a consent or privacy checkbox for a company you have not approved once, never answers a screening question with anything outside the truth file and answers bank, never claims a skill, a year or a number that is not in the master resume, never applies to a posting that fails a hard filter (citizenship, clearance, onsite, W2-only when you are C2C), and never exceeds the daily cap. When it hits any of these it stops, says why, and waits. Reviewing these refusals is part of the daily two minutes.

**Honesty is also the strategy.** Every screening answer is checked against documents at the I-9 and background-check stage; a generous "years with Snowflake" is found in the first technical screen. The agent's value is volume with consistency, not creativity with facts.

**Daily caps and pacing.** Ten to fifteen tailored applications a day is a strong pace; the kit's default cap is 15 and the LinkedIn share of that should stay under 25 a day even if you raise it. Space the submissions (the skill waits a random 20–60 seconds between applications and does not run overnight) so the pattern looks like a person working, because it is one — you are reviewing and approving.

**Terms of service, plainly.** The job boards' terms were written for scrapers and auto-apply bots, and parts of them apply to any automation, including this one, so decide with open eyes:

- *LinkedIn's User Agreement* (section 8.2, "Don'ts") prohibits using bots or other automated methods to access the Services, and software, scripts, browser plug-ins or add-ons that scrape or automate the Services; LinkedIn's help center lists "prohibited software and extensions" and says accounts using them can be restricted. Claude in Chrome acts in your logged-in browser at human pace and under your supervision, which is a materially different thing from a headless auto-apply bot, but it is still automation under that wording. Review mode, the daily cap, the pauses, and never touching your profile or messaging are what keep the account looking like a person using LinkedIn — because it is. If LinkedIn ever shows a verification or restriction notice, stop the agent on LinkedIn for a week and apply by hand there.
- *Indeed's Terms of Service* (job-seeker section, updated 29 September 2026) state that use of automation, scripting or bots to automate the Indeed Apply process outside Indeed's official vendors and tooling is prohibited. That is why the kit does not submit on Indeed: it scouts there, prefills in review mode, and you click.
- *Dice* publishes no comparable clause we could find; its terms still prohibit misuse and scraping, so the same human-pace rules apply.
- *Company ATS forms* (Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, Workday) are the employer's own forms; filling them with a browser assistant and submitting after your review is no different from using an autofill, and this is where the agent should do most of its work.

**Data and privacy.** The truth file holds your phone number, address and visa status; the tracker holds every company you have applied to. Keep `~/job-search` out of any shared cloud folder and out of public git repos (`.gitignore` is in the kit). The Gmail connector reads your mailbox: scope it with labels as described above, or use a dedicated address. Claude Code's own data handling is described in Anthropic's documentation; the browser extension's permissions are per site and visible in its settings.

**When to turn auto mode on.** After at least a week in review mode with no corrections needed on screening answers, no two-page failures, and a tracker that matches LinkedIn's *Applied* list exactly. Switch it on for Dice and company ATS forms first; keep LinkedIn in review mode longer, because that is where the account risk lives. Switch it off again the day anything looks wrong.
