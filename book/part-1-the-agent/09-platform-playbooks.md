# 9. Platform playbooks

Each playbook is a file under the `job-apply` skill (`references/linkedin.md`, `dice.md`, `indeed.md`, `ats.md`, plus a small page helper, `linkedin-helper.js`); the search URLs live under `job-scout` (`references/search-urls.md`). The agent reads the one it needs before its first use in a session. What follows is the human version, with the lessons that cost the most time.

**LinkedIn Easy Apply.** Search with the filters in the URL so the run is reproducible: Easy Apply on (`f_AL=true`), remote (`f_WT=2`), posted within your freshness window (`f_TPR=r172800` for 48 hours), job type contract or full-time as your profile accepts (`f_JT=C`, `F`, or both), United States (`geoId=103644278`), sorted by date (`sortBy=DD`). The scout reads the result list, then opens each candidate at its canonical `/jobs/view/<id>/` URL to capture the *About the job* text for screening; the apply step opens the same URL in a new tab and clicks Easy Apply. A plain *Apply* button leaves LinkedIn and is handled as a company ATS form (it does not count toward the LinkedIn cap). The dialog has three to six steps: contact (prefilled by LinkedIn), resume (it uploads the tailored PDF — the file input is hidden, so the skill's helper script exposes it first — and makes sure the new file, not an older upload, is selected), screening questions (radio buttons, dropdowns, numeric *years of* fields and short text; answered from the bank and the skill-years table), then *Review* and *Submit application*. Two details matter: untick **Follow company** on the review step (it is on by default), and if LinkedIn offers to update your profile, save the resume or turn on alerts after submitting, choose *Not now* — the profile is never edited. LinkedIn throttles: roughly 25–35 Easy Apply submissions a day is where friction begins; the kit's LinkedIn ceiling (25) and overall cap (15) keep you below it, and at any daily-limit or "unusual activity" notice the agent stops on LinkedIn for the day. Postings that say *W2 only*, *US citizens only* or *onsite* (when they clash with your profile) are skipped by the scout's filters or flagged as blockers by the screener; repeated postings of the same client role by several staffing agencies count once — the scout keeps the best source (Easy Apply or the direct employer) and records the rest as skipped duplicates. After each submission the agent checks that the job appears in LinkedIn's *Applied* list before it records `submitted`; compare that list with the tracker yourself once a week.

**Dice.** Dice is the strongest US board for contract and corp-to-corp AI work and the easiest to automate because its form is short. Search for your titles with *Remote* and a recent posted date, filtered by employment type. The flow is: open the posting, *Easy apply*, upload the tailored PDF in place of the stored resume (if Dice says that replaces the resume on your profile, the agent asks you once per session), attach the cover letter only if asked, answer the employer's questions if there are any, review, submit, and confirm the *Applied* badge on the job page. A posting that sends you to the employer's site follows the ATS rules below. Dice shows the recruiter's name and the posting's employment type (C2C, W2, 1099) up front — the screener reads it and flags mismatches before you waste a tailored resume, and the agent copies the recruiter's contact into the tracker notes (it never messages or calls anyone). Recruiters on Dice call fast; keep the phone on and the rate answer ready.

**Indeed.** Indeed's terms prohibit automating Indeed Apply outside Indeed's own tools, so the agent never submits on Indeed, in review or auto mode. It uses Indeed for discovery (alerts plus search, *Remote*, *Last 24 hours*), scores postings, and for those you queue it prepares the package and prefills the Indeed Apply form with the tailored resume and the bank's answers, then sets the row to `ready`; you press *Submit your application*. In practice most Indeed postings link to the employer's own site, which is the better route anyway: the agent follows *Apply on company site* and uses the ATS playbook below. Indeed's own resume profile should be complete because many employers see it before the PDF.

**Company ATS forms (Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, Workday, iCIMS, SmartRecruiters).** These are the applications that convert best because they reach the hiring team directly. Greenhouse (`boards.greenhouse.io/<company>` or `job-boards.greenhouse.io`), Lever (`jobs.lever.co/<company>`) and Ashby (`jobs.ashbyhq.com/<company>`) are one-page forms: name, email, phone, resume upload, LinkedIn URL, a few custom questions, work-authorization questions, and the EEO block (which the bank answers *decline to self-identify*). The agent fills them in under a minute, re-checks whatever the form autofilled from the resume, and then follows the mode: in review mode it stops before *Submit application*, in auto mode it submits within the caps. It never uses "Apply with LinkedIn", "Apply with Indeed" or "Apply with Google" buttons, which share profile data or create accounts. Workday is the exception: it requires an account per employer, an email verification and a multi-page profile, so the agent does not start it at all — it marks the row `needs-check` ("Workday: account required, apply manually") and gives you the link (creating accounts is on its never-do list). iCIMS, Taleo, SmartRecruiters and the other systems follow the general rules, and most of them stop early: at any account, email verification code or CAPTCHA the agent stops, marks the row `needs-check` and fills nothing more. Two rules for all ATS forms: a consent checkbox that accepts a privacy policy is ticked only if `approved_consents` in your profile lists that company and that consent (when you approve one during a review-mode run, the agent records it there first), and *"How did you hear about us?"* is answered truthfully (*LinkedIn*, *Indeed*, *Dice*, *company site*).

**What the tracker records for every platform**: id, platform, company, role, posting URL, apply URL, location, employment type, rate or salary if stated, fit score, status, the dates found and applied, the resume and cover files used, the follow-up date, and notes. The screening answers the agent expected to give are in the posting's `answers.md`. If anything on a form had to be guessed, was unclear or could not be verified, the row becomes `needs-check` with the reason in notes, and `/job-tracker` lists it.
