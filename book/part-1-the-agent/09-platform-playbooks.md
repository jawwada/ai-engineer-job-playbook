# 9. Platform playbooks

Each playbook is a file under the `job-apply` skill (`references/linkedin.md`, `dice.md`, `indeed.md`, `ats.md`). The agent reads the one it needs when it needs it. What follows is the human version, with the lessons that cost us the most time.

**LinkedIn Easy Apply.** Search with the filters in the URL so the run is reproducible: Easy Apply on (`f_AL=true`), remote (`f_WT=2`), posted in the last 48 hours (`f_TPR=r172800`), job type contract or full-time as you prefer (`f_JT=C` or `F`), United States (`geoId=103644278`), sorted by date (`sortBy=DD`). The agent opens each card, reads the *About the job* text, scores it, and for queued jobs clicks Easy Apply. The modal has three to six steps: contact (prefilled by LinkedIn), resume (it uploads the tailored PDF — the file input is hidden, so the skill exposes it before the upload), screening questions (radio buttons, dropdowns, numeric *years of* fields and short text; answered from the bank and the skill-years table), then *Review* and *Submit application*. Two details matter: untick **Follow company** on the review step (it is on by default), and if LinkedIn pops its *"Would you like to add this to your profile?"* prompt after submitting, choose *Not now* — the profile is never edited. LinkedIn throttles: roughly 25–35 Easy Apply submissions a day is where friction begins; the cap in `profile.yaml` keeps you below it. Postings that say *W2 only*, *US citizens only*, or *onsite* are skipped at the screener; so are repeated postings of the same role by several staffing agencies — the screener keeps the earliest. Verify the day's work once a week in **Jobs → My jobs → Applied**; the tracker should match it exactly.

**Dice.** Dice is the strongest US board for contract and corp-to-corp AI work and the easiest to automate because its form is short. Search for your titles with *Remote* and *Posted today/last 3 days*; Dice marks postings with *Easy Apply*. The flow is: open the posting, Easy Apply, confirm or replace the resume (upload the tailored PDF), answer two to five questions (work authorization, employment type, rate), submit. Dice shows the recruiter's name and the posting's employment type (C2C, W2, 1099) up front — the screener reads it and skips mismatches before you waste a tailored resume. Recruiters on Dice call fast; keep the phone on and the rate answer ready.

**Indeed.** Indeed's terms prohibit automating Indeed Apply outside Indeed's own tools, so the agent does not submit on Indeed. It uses Indeed for discovery (alerts plus search, *Remote*, *Last 24 hours*), scores postings, and for those you want it prepares the package and, in review mode, prefills the Indeed Apply form with the tailored resume and the bank's answers; you press *Submit your application*. In practice most Indeed postings link to the employer's own site, which is the better route anyway: the agent follows *Apply on company site* and uses the ATS playbook below. Indeed's own resume profile should be complete because many employers see it before the PDF.

**Company ATS forms (Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, Workday, iCIMS, SmartRecruiters).** These are the applications that convert best because they reach the hiring team directly. Greenhouse (`boards.greenhouse.io/<company>` or `job-boards.greenhouse.io`), Lever (`jobs.lever.co/<company>`) and Ashby (`jobs.ashbyhq.com/<company>`) are one-page forms: name, email, phone, resume upload, LinkedIn URL, a few custom questions, work-authorization questions, and the EEO block (which the bank answers *decline to self-identify*). The agent fills them in under a minute and stops before *Submit application*. Workday is the exception: it requires an account per employer, an email verification and a multi-page profile; the agent stops at *Create account* and hands it to you (creating accounts is on its never-do list), then can continue once you are logged in. iCIMS and SmartRecruiters sit in between — the agent handles them but expects a CAPTCHA now and then. Two rules for all ATS forms: consent checkboxes that accept a privacy policy are ticked only after you have said yes once for that company (the kit remembers your answer per domain), and *"How did you hear about us?"* is answered truthfully (*LinkedIn*, *Indeed*, *Dice*, *company site*).

**What the tracker records for every platform**: id, platform, company, role, posting URL, apply URL, employment type, rate or salary if stated, date applied, resume and cover files used, screening answers given, the status, and the follow-up date. If anything on a form had to be guessed, the row carries a `needs-check` note and the daily summary lists it.
